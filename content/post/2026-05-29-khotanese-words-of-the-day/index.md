I think it might be during one of my sightseeing tours to a translated Khotanese texts that I encountered the word *mijṣe'* 'lady'. The word is remarkable for containing the extremely marginal kāra and phoneme *jṣ* /dʐ/. A cursory tour of the major reference works and summary articles on Khotanese would suffice to lend one the impression of the phoneme's rarity.

In the TITUS corpus, we find *jṣ* in 159 words (39 distinct), out of a grand total of 180~200k[^2]. Among these are:
- *siṃjṣīṃji*, an obvious misspelling of *siṃjsīṃji* '4th month in the Khotanese calendar' with the unremarkable phoneme *js* /dz/. Whether this form is to be attributed to the retypist, printer, typist, transcriber, or the mediaeval scribe is something worth looking into.
- various inflections and spelling variants of *mijṣe'* (25, 13 dist.)
- a form *ā:jṣyāṃ* which I take to be related to the one immediately following
- various words beginning with *jṣā(ṁ)ñ-*, *jṣe'ñ-* etc. (131, 22 dist.). Most frequent of these is *jṣāṁ'ñāṁñä* (63x) and *jṣāṁñāṁña* (30x), found in KT1: Siddhasāra and Jīvakapustaka (two medical compendia), as well as KT3.34 (P 2893).
- *jṣe*, whose possible connection with *jṣā(ṁ)ñ-* was not apparent to me.

Bailey's 1979 Dictionary of Khotan Saka, still **the** '*Dict.*' when it comes to the language despite its shortcomings, the ghostwords that subsequent works would point out, and the fact that it is yet to be fully digitalised (this I say with no intent against the late professor's memory), does not contain an entry beginning in jṣ, and I think I might have missed the revealing entry (see below) in a search. And so I planned to conduct a reading of this part of the JS and other texts where the etymon surfaces; an excellent occassion for a translation exercise.

A chance encounter of an entry in p. 113 saved me the trouble (but alas deprived me of the aforesaid occassion, but worry not I have other translations in mind). **jṣe** redirects to **jīṣ-** 'to boil', whose entry is to be found in p. 110. And lo and behold we find , including the oft-repeated jṣā(ṃ)ñāñ-[^3]:

"basic" forms
- *jīṣ-* (attested 3sg. *jīṣdi*) : '(the carrot) is boiling' 

causatives
- *jṣāñ-*&#9;basic : 'I am boiling (the carrot)'
- *jṣāñ- āñ-*&#9;neccesitative : 'to be / should be / supposed to be boiled ( = Latin *-endum* )'

The form jṣe seems in want of explanation: if *jīṣdi* is the 3sg.pres., what is *jṣe*?

Hitch (2016) reminded me that N. Sims-Williams makes note of *mijṣe* and *jṣ* in *Studies III* ("reminded", since I am sure I have encountered the same entry long ago). And lo and behold (again!) I discovered, to my delight, that N. Sims-Williams has made note of the "[other] Khotanese word containing jṣ", something I may have missed or forgotten.

And thus the curtain closes on a word we would just expect to occur in materia medica.
